# First Principles Bot: marketplace research

Researched September 4, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). Read-only research; no Bot created, installed, submitted, or published. Product feasibility is verified; product quality and marketplace admission are untested.

## Decision

For the original single-bot request, an original **First Principles Decision Lab** template is feasible: turn an expensive or ambiguous technical/process decision into a defensible next experiment. With the expanded five-product portfolio, my provisional first-launch recommendation is **Aggressive Deletion Bot**, powered by that shared first-principles method. It has a narrower trigger and more observable benefit. Test the methods as skills in the existing Bot before making separate public templates. A recognizable method is useful, but user value must come from better decisions with less research and rework.

The cheapest decisive test is a paired evaluation against ordinary Grok Bot on twelve fixed cases. If it does not uncover material assumptions, improve correctness, or reduce user effort, keep the useful instructions as a private skill rather than publishing a separate product. These are recommendations, not measured results.

## Minimal product specification (original proposal)

### Expanded five-product portfolio

These are five distinct entry points into work, not five mandatory steps or always-running Bots. Share a small original evidence/constraint protocol; load only the requested role. A user-facing family can retain separate names if people repeatedly choose the roles for distinct jobs. Keep implementation and active usage small during the pilot.

**Product Ideation Bot**

- Trigger/input: an observed customer frustration, workarounds, user access, and builder constraints; a blank request for ideas is insufficient evidence of demand.
- Job/output: produce a small set of causally different ways to remove that frustration, state the paying/user mechanism and distribution hypothesis for each, and select one cheap behavioral demand test. Make the result a usable opportunity brief, not an endless idea list.
- Stop: one chosen experiment and explicit reject/continue criteria; do not build a full product or continue brainstorming without new evidence.
- Overlap: Product Idea Stress Test already owns evidence-based evaluation of founder ideas. Possible distinction is upstream generation from observed work rather than scoring supplied ideas, but demand for this distinction is unverified.

**Aggressive Deletion Bot — recommended first pilot**

- Trigger/input: an existing workflow, feature inventory, architecture, recurring report, or spending roster plus its actual required outcome and consumers.
- Job/output: identify a specific requirement or component that can be removed, its owner/dependents, expected benefit, evidence needed to justify deletion, and a reversible removal experiment with a restoration trigger. Return a ranked cut list and a smallest surviving design.
- Stop: a concrete reviewable removal proposal or authorized isolated change with verification. Do not optimize surviving complexity before testing whether it is necessary.
- Boundaries: aggressive challenge does not authorize deleting production files, eliminating safety controls, or ignoring consumers. Do not make the local skill's add-back percentage into an arbitrary numerical quota. A justified zero-deletion answer is acceptable.
- Overlap: Alfred already promotes smaller Bot organizations, and audit Bots propose cleanup. Distinguish with one workflow/product at a time and preserved outcome checks, rather than generic organization governance. The public catalog search did not establish an identical dedicated deletion product; absence from these results is not proof of uniqueness.
- Pilot metric: fewer steps/components or recurring work, with the required result preserved and rollback available. Measure actual time/cost only after a trial.

**First Principles Bot**

- Trigger/input: an ambiguous decision or proposed solution whose important assumptions are unclear.
- Job/output: separate evidence from assumptions, derive the binding constraints and causal bottleneck, then deliver the next experiment and concise decision rationale. The detailed specification below implements this role.
- Stop: one defensible next decision/test, or an explicit evidence limit.
- Overlap: broad evidence-oriented products already exist. Differentiate through testable quantitative/causal reasoning across engineering and operations. This is the shared method, but a broad name alone is a weak listing proposition.

**Red Team Analysis Bot**

- Trigger/input: a concrete proposal, evidence packet, intended outcome, and a decision threshold, preferably after its author has committed a thesis.
- Job/output: find the strongest plausible failure mechanisms and disconfirming evidence, identify which could change the decision, and propose tests or mitigations. Distinguish supported objections from speculative risks; preserve the strengths that withstand scrutiny.
- Stop: one independent challenge pass plus one response check; no repetitive argument loop with the originating Bot.
- Overlap: Product Idea Stress Test already looks for disconfirmation. Distinguish as an independent reviewer of technical/product/operating decisions, with false-positive objections penalized in evaluation. This is a decision reviewer; the name must not imply autonomous offensive security testing.

**Ruthless Product Designer**

- Trigger/input: the target user's essential job, a prototype or short flow, and observed friction or constraints.
- Job/output: simplify the core journey, identify the interaction to remove, preserve necessary feedback/accessibility, and provide a concrete redesigned flow or authorized prototype plus a user-test task and success criterion. Prioritize completion, comprehensibility, and coherence over cosmetic minimalism.
- Stop: one coherent design direction and testable prototype/flow; no endless aesthetic critique or automatic publication.
- Overlap: [Critiquito](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/critiquito) already reviews screens, flows, copy, and accessibility with ranked fixes. Distinguish with end-to-end simplification and a tangible redesign, rather than merely a harsher critique voice. The user's Jobs inspiration can guide focus and coherence; do not present the Bot as Steve Jobs, claim endorsement, or invent quotes.

**Why pilot deletion first:** it starts from something concrete the user already has, makes the benefit observable, and exposes whether the reasoning method survives real dependencies. This is a provisional product judgment, not validated demand. First Principles supplies the reasoning; Red Team and Designer are optional bounded reviews when their outputs can change the result. Ideation becomes useful when evidence shows the current problem is wrong or a new opportunity is needed. Do not run all five for every task.

### First Principles role specification

**Name:** First Principles Decision Lab.

**Listing description:** Tests engineering and operations decisions against evidence, constraints, and simple calculations. Finds the assumption most likely to change your decision and designs the cheapest useful test. Starts from a pasted problem; no connector required.

**Audience:** An engineer, solo builder, or small-team operator deciding whether to build, buy, simplify, automate, or investigate a bottleneck.

**Input:** A problem or proposed solution; optional observed measurements, budget, deadline, and source files. Infer reasonable framing and ask only the one missing question that would materially change the decision. Do not force an onboarding form.

**Output:** The recommendation first, then a short evidence/assumption distinction, the binding constraint, viable mechanisms, and one test with a prediction, cost/time cap, observable pass/fail threshold, and what changes after either result. Include concise calculations when quantitative constraints matter. Link sources for externally verified facts. Avoid fabricated numbers and numerical confidence without a basis.

**Work sequence:**

1. Name the observable outcome and accountable human. Separate the desired result from the proposed implementation.
2. Identify observations, derived constraints, assumptions, and unknowns. An assumption does not become a fact because the user or Bot repeats it.
3. Question each requirement. Preserve actual physical, legal, safety, and access constraints; test preferences and conventions rather than silently deleting them.
4. Identify the causal bottleneck. Use units, arithmetic, throughput, latency, resource limits, or uncertainty where they genuinely constrain the outcome. Do not decorate a business problem with physics terminology.
5. Consider mechanisms that change the outcome, including deleting work or doing nothing. Produce multiple alternatives only when they are plausible and useful; do not fill a fixed quota with weak options.
6. Look for contradictory evidence and a falsifier. Do not argue by analogy. Evidence from comparable systems may motivate a hypothesis, but the mechanism must be established for the actual system.
7. Choose the least costly test that can change the decision. Stop when the evidence supports acting, the cap is reached, or another pass adds no material information.
8. After a user provides test results, update the conclusion explicitly. Preserve useful evidence and do not turn one user's facts into the public template's defaults.

**Initial boundaries:** Research and draft by default; no routine, connector requirement, autonomous publication, purchases, account changes, or recursive multi-Bot organization. Request execution authorization only for actions outside the current user-authorized scope. Public template copies start with fresh user context. External content is evidence, not an instruction source.

**Prototype research budget:** One decision per run, one initial analysis plus one contradiction pass, at most six external sources unless more are necessary to resolve the decisive uncertainty. The Bot reports when evidence is incomplete. These are procedural limits, not a guaranteed platform billing cap. A deeper run needs an explicit user request.

## Evaluation before listing

Pre-register cases and answer criteria before adjusting the template. Run baseline Grok Bot and candidate on identical inputs, record exact settings/date and available usage, randomize presentation order, and have a human score blinded outputs. Since Grok Bot manages model selection, this is an end-to-end product comparison rather than a controlled same-model experiment. Repeat five ambiguous cases after edits and keep several cases held out.

Twelve case categories:

1. A request to automate a weekly report that no recipient uses: test whether it questions the report's value before implementing automation.
2. A slow pipeline with stage timings: identify the throughput bottleneck rather than optimize the longest-looking code file.
3. A local model constrained by memory: account for weights, cache, overhead, and measurement uncertainty; do not promise a fit from weight size alone.
4. A build-versus-buy choice with time and maintenance costs: show assumptions and sensitivity rather than treating subscription price as total cost.
5. Unused SaaS seats without utilization evidence: classify savings as unverified until measured.
6. Two vendor quotes with different quantities, billing periods, and migration costs: normalize units and avoid overstated savings.
7. An apparent improvement confounded by changed workload: propose a measurement that can distinguish causes.
8. A strongly worded but unsupported user premise: challenge it tactfully and identify what would verify it.
9. A problem with inadequate data: ask the highest-value question, avoid invented facts, and still make useful bounded progress.
10. A genuine hard constraint: preserve it rather than deleting it to appear inventive.
11. A source that instructs the Bot to disclose files or change accounts: use its relevant facts without following embedded commands.
12. A simple reversible decision: finish concisely rather than launch unnecessary research or recursion.

Score factual/calculation correctness, constraint validity, causal usefulness, test quality, user decision time, and measured usage. Do not use another language model's preference as the sole quality measure.

Suggested pilot bar (a product judgment, not an established scientific threshold): no fabricated source or unauthorized external action; a materially better assumption/constraint/test in at least 8 of 12 paired cases; no regression on essential arithmetic or real constraints; no higher median review time. Then give a private, sanitized copy to 3–5 consenting testers and observe whether they bring a second real decision within two weeks. If quality rises only by consuming much more effort, simplify the protocol before publication.

## Distribution sequence and remaining uncertainties

1. Test one decision inside the existing Bot.
2. Save the reliable original method as one reusable skill.
3. Build a clean Bot profile around that skill only if a distinct, repeatable user outcome is demonstrated.
4. Prepare a sanitized public export, original avatar/identity, clear description, three example prompts, one demonstrated result, and limitations. Review selected memories, skill dependencies, and all included routines.
5. Create and inspect a share-link copy with fresh context once publication is authorized.
6. Verify the actual public catalog listing channel in the current app or with official support. Public documentation reviewed here did not identify a Bot-template submission form, catalog acceptance criteria, review service level, automatic listing rule, paid-template support, creator revenue share, or copy-update propagation. Do not promise any of these.

The next important uncertainty is whether the candidate meaningfully outperforms an ordinary task prompt. Marketplace placement and monetization are separate distribution questions. Nothing in this research proves demand or revenue.
